The common assumption

"The litigation exemption covers us." Only partly.

Section 17(1)(a) covers

  • Processing necessary to enforce a legal right or claim
  • Pleadings, evidence, witness data
  • Opposing party's records

It may not cover

  • Advisory, drafting, due diligence
  • Website, enquiries, newsletters
  • Billing and your own staff's data
  • Data kept after it stops being needed
Even where the exemption applies, Section 8(5) security safeguards and accountability for vendors (8(1)) still apply.

Preview · Breach Response Playbook

A laptop with client files is stolen. What now?

Hour 0–1
Call the partner on call. Don't delete anything. Note the time you found out.
Hour 0–2
Contain: remote wipe, reset passwords, revoke shared links.
Without delay
Intimate the Data Protection Board and each affected person in plain language.
72 hours
Send the detailed report to the Board: facts, cause, mitigation, notices given.

60-second self-check

Can your chambers answer yes to all three?

1
Does every email account used for client work have two-step verification?
2
Is client data on WhatsApp kept only on firm devices or a business number?
3
If a laptop is stolen tonight, do you know who calls whom and what goes to the Board within 72 hours?
If any answer is "no", that is where your DPDP work starts.
