Most law firms will tell their clients about the DPDP Act.

Very few have checked their own office.

Think about a normal day in chambers:
→ Client sends Aadhaar and case papers on an associate's personal WhatsApp
→ Drafts go to the typist on a pen drive
→ A Google Drive folder is shared as "anyone with the link"
→ An intern pastes client facts into a free AI tool
→ 10 years of closed files sit in a store room

Under the DPDP Act, 2023, a law firm is a Data Fiduciary like any other business.

Many advocates assume the litigation exemption in Section 17(1)(a) covers them. It does not cover everything:
• It applies only where processing is necessary to enforce a legal right or claim.
• Advisory, transactional and due diligence work may fall outside it.
• So do your website, billing and your own staff's data.
• The security duty under Section 8(5) still applies, even to exempt data. Failing it carries the highest penalty in the Act: up to ₹250 crore.

The core obligations under the DPDP Rules, 2025 apply from May 2027. That is about six months away.

A quick self-check for any chambers:
1. Does every email account used for client work have two-step verification?
2. Are client files on WhatsApp only on firm devices?
3. Do you know whom to call, and what to send the Data Protection Board, within 72 hours if a laptop is stolen?
If any answer is "no", start there.
